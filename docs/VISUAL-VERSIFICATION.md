# Visual versification — the sprocket, the chain, and the Easter egg

**Design settled with Howell at the bench, 2026-08-03.** Nothing here is
built. This exists because the whole design lived in one conversation and
would otherwise have been lost.

## The metaphor, which is already literally true on screen

The wheel is a **sprocket**, and the ring of nodes is a **chain** wrapped
partially around it. In the primary stratum the reader turns it with a thumb
and the links move; entering dimension mode reveals the primary for what it
has always been — a sprocket and a chain, not merely a wheel.

The disc is **the scripture**. That is not a metaphor imposed on the picture,
it is what the picture already shows: the disc is where the words render.
The chain of numbered links wrapped around it is **the addressing** — chapter
and verse, laid over the text by human editors.

Which makes the instrument a drawing of the utterance model itself. Engagement
means one address per utterance. **A chain that only ever meshes perfectly is
a chain claiming that versification is intrinsic to scripture — which is the
one thing we know to be false.**

## The artistic licence, and why it is the truest part

The disc does not rotate. Mechanically that means the chain is *permanently
slipping*, and Howell took the licence knowingly: a crown of thorns turning
with every swipe would become intolerable within a day.

But the licence is not a compromise. **A chain that slips forever over a
fixed gear IS versification** — the text sits still, and the numbering never
quite keeps step with it. Were the disc to turn in perfect mesh, the
instrument would assert that chapter and verse belong to scripture. It does
not, and they do not.

## The three types, and their three motions

Each is a different fact, so each earns a different motion. Using one motion
for all three would flatten the very distinction the model exists to draw.
In all three the **disc never moves**: scripture does not change, only the
numbering does.

**1 · Lost utterances.** The words are not at another address; they are not
there. Greek 1 Samuel 17:12–31 do not exist in the Septuagint. Entering the
Greek from an edition that has them, the chain separates from the disc, those
links migrate off screen, the gap closes, and the chain settles back against
the disc. Reversed, the chain opens to make room and the links arrive.

**2 · Re-addressed utterances.** The same words under a different number.
The chain must move relative to the disc. Because the lens anchors the
reader's **utterance** and not their number, the chain slides *under* the
lens: the reader has not moved, and the number over their words changes from
32 to 34. The lift and the settle travel the chain as a **ripple**; the
translation is what happens while the chain is free. (Howell offered the
ripple and the lift-and-move as alternatives; they compose — the ripple is
*how* it disengages, the slide is *what* it does while disengaged.)

**3 · Split and merged utterances.** The words are present in both editions
and cut differently. **Nothing may enter or leave the frame** — the words
were on screen the whole time, inside their neighbour. The chain disengages,
one link splits into two (the second emerging from the first) or two slide
together into one, and the chain re-engages. This is the distinction between
*absent* and *differently counted*, and it must not be blurred into a fly-in.

## Rules

- **The disc never moves.** Every motion belongs to the chain.
- **The lens anchors the utterance, never the number.** The reader does not
  move; the numbering moves around them.
- **Changes occur anywhere along the visible chain**, not only at the lens.
  If 17:32 is under the lens with 11 above it, the chain opens *above* the
  lens to receive what is arriving.
- **Direction follows the established two-flock grammar** (Howell,
  2026-07-24), not the level-change motion. Up-chain kin arrive from above
  the upper-left corner, down-chain kin from the lower tangent past the
  lower-right, assigned by chain order alone. Lower-left is the *level*
  change — book names surrendering to chapters — and versification is a
  same-level event: the chain stays the verse chain and merely lengthens or
  shortens. Sending everything to lower-left would make a verse destined
  above the lens swim across the whole chain to reach its seat.
- **One lift, all changes, one settle.** Several changes can land in the
  visible window at once; if each disengages on its own schedule the ring
  reads as malfunctioning.
- **No performance under `convention: true`.** Judith and Tobit share
  coordinates with the Latin by convention only. Any of these motions asserts
  a correspondence, and there it would be a lie.

## It is an Easter egg, and that is a design constraint

Howell's framing: all of this would become annoying were versification not
rare. Measured against the Greek charts — 246 fused seats and 188 skipped
runs against 29,133 seats, about 1.5%, and only ever seen when an edition
changes. Three things follow.

- **Rarity licenses slowness.** Interface animation is usually quick because
  it repeats; this may be seen a handful of times ever, so it can take a
  deliberate beat.
- **It must be purely additive.** An Easter egg cannot be load-bearing: if it
  does not run, the reader must still land correctly. E2's re-seat is already
  correct and complete without any of this, which is also why this can safely
  be the last thing built.
- **Only the neighbourhood animates.** Some 368 utterances differ between
  Greek and the spine; a reader switching editions in Genesis sees the dozen
  links around them. That is not a limitation — an Easter egg is something
  you happen to witness, and no full accounting is owed.

## Open

- Whether the reader's own node flying away (standing on Latin 17:20,
  switching to Greek) reads as honest or as loss. Howell approved it; it
  wants seeing before believing.
- Whether this grammar generalises beyond scripture. Other volumes have no
  versification, but they do have membership changes (the search prune
  already flies nodes away), so the disengage-lift may be general vocabulary
  rather than a Bible-only dialect.
- Frequency. Rare for a reader, but Howell switches editions more than
  anyone, and every launch passes the funnel. He is the canary: if it starts
  to feel like a tic rather than a discovery, that is information about
  frequency, not about the design.
